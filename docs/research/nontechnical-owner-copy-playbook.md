# Outcome-Language Copy Playbook: Selling a Free 15-Minute Session to Non-Technical Small Business Owners

## 1. The 8-12 highest-impact VERBATIM phrases to mirror

Pulled from real owner speech (Reddit, surveys, founder writing). Use these near-verbatim in headlines, subheads, and the "sound like you get it" opening. They beat any paraphrase.

1. **"We don't own a small business — it owns us."** *(commenter reply on the viral coffee-shop Reddit post; the single strongest line — inverts the dream of ownership)*
2. **"I used to like coffee. Now it just haunts my dreams."** *(the owner's own words — visceral, self-deprecating)*
3. **"It's a constant juggling act."** *(staffing, inventory, marketing, compliance, paperwork)*
4. **"I've become the most expensive employee I ever hired."** *(Alan Pentz)*
5. **"I'm the human duct tape holding everything together."** *(Pentz)*
6. **"My vacation requires a 47-point handoff doc."** *(Pentz)*
7. **"It's faster if I just do it myself."** *(the real reason owners don't delegate — 25% in Time etc survey)*
8. **"Sometimes there's so much work it's hard to get it all done as one person."**
9. **"I needed to be at work for my company to run."** *(Walmart Business survey — 41%)*
10. **"I never fully switch off."** *(62% of owners) / "I always feel tired."* *(49%)*
11. **"I'm spending all my time IN the business and none of it ON it."**
12. **"I started this for freedom and it feels more burdensome than the 9-5 I left."**

**For the AI/tech-fear layer specifically:** "That's for the big guys, not for me." · "I'm not techy." · "I just want my weekends back." · "I tried ChatGPT and it gave me beige content that sounded like a polite robot." · "Is this just a sales pitch?"

**Rule:** lead with their words, not your features. Open the page with the pain in their language, then introduce the call as the way out.

## 2. The owner outcomes that sell hardest — and how to phrase them

Owners don't buy software, automation, or websites. They buy **escape from a nameable pain + the emotional state on the other side.** ~95% of the buying decision is emotional (directional, not gospel — Zaltman). Lead with the felt outcome, justify with the feature underneath.

Ranked by selling power:

| Outcome (what they actually want) | How to phrase it | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Peace of mind / one less thing to worry about** | "One less thing to worry about." / "Stop checking your phone at dinner." | #1 reason owners pay to outsource — beat expertise and time-savings in the Paychex data (40% peace-of-mind vs 23% expertise vs 19% time; **note: 2015 payroll-specific data — directional**). It's an emotional *terminal state*, not a feature. |
| **Stop losing money you can't see** | "Stop losing the jobs you never knew came in." / "Find the money you're leaving on the table." | Loss framing beats gain framing (~21-30% lift in cited tests, though the missed-call dollar stats are vendor-aggregated estimates — use as *illustrative*, not hard data). The leak is invisible, so naming it + dollarizing it creates urgency "more revenue" can't. |
| **Get your time back — to grow** | "Get your evenings back." / "Win back a full workday a week — to spend it ON the business, not IN it." | Must tie to revenue/life, NOT leisure. The 68%/32% IN-vs-ON split is the concrete version of "no time." Frame reclaimed time as "time to do the work that actually grows the business." |
| **Sleep at night / stop the financial panic** | "Stop losing sleep over the gap between money coming in and bills going out." | Literal, not figurative — 68% of owners lose a full night's sleep a month to money worry (Bluevine); the timing gap (41%) beats payroll and taxes as the top stressor. |
| **Grow without hiring** | "Do more without the cost, risk, and HR headache of another hire." | Owners refuse headcount but are drowning. Frame automation as leverage, NOT layoffs ("this doesn't replace anyone"). |
| **Look legit / stop losing customers who assume you're not** | "Stop losing customers who assume you're not still in business." | Loss-framed, not vanity-framed. (Design-credibility percentages are directional — older Stanford-lineage stats heavily repeated by vendors.) |

**The mechanism for writing any of these:** take a feature, ask "So what?" repeatedly until you hit an emotional terminal outcome.
> *"Auto text-back → so you never miss a job → so you stop losing ~$500/mo → so you can finally stop checking your phone at dinner."*
> Put the deepest rung in the headline. Keep the feature as proof underneath.

## 3. Jargon-to-outcome translation table (kill list → say this instead)

Delete the technology from the sentence. The brittle words trigger the three deal-killers: *it'll be complicated, expensive, and not for a business like mine.*

| ❌ Kill (triggers fear) | ✅ Say instead (names the job/outcome) |
|---|---|
| workflow / pipeline / integration | "I connect the tools you already use so they talk to each other" |
| automation | "doing the boring, repeating jobs for you" |
| AI agent | "a digital assistant that handles one task start to finish" |
| LLM / model / GenAI | "the same kind of tech behind ChatGPT" |
| implementation / onboarding | "I get it running for you, usually within a couple of weeks" |
| "I'll build you an automated workflow" | "Every missed call gets a text back within 60 seconds — and turns into a booked job" |
| optimize / streamline / efficiencies | "stop doing [the specific chore they named] by hand" |
| "leverage synergistic solutions" | "I'll fix the thing that's costing you sales" |
| inefficient workflows / lack of automation | their words: "the admin that eats your evenings" / "the human duct tape problem" |
| "AI-powered platform" | "a tireless assistant that does the boring work your team hates" |

**Two anchoring rules:**
- **Anchor every claim to ONE specific recurring chore the owner named**, in their words ("posting on Instagram?" "chasing late invoices?"), then describe the boring-work-gone outcome, then attach a number.
- **Quantify with time AND emotion:** not "save 10 hours," but **"win back a full workday every week — your weekends are yours again."** (business.com: avg ~5.6 hrs saved/employee/week — directional.)

## 4. Recommended offer name/framing — DROP "audit"

**Decision: replace "audit."** Reasoning:

- For a non-technical owner, "audit" carries an IRS/tax-scrutiny frame — *"we'll inspect you and find what you did wrong."* That raises defensiveness instead of desire. (This risk is real when the offer sits near anything financial/compliance-flavored; "audit" is fine for SEO/web lead magnets, so the effect is context-dependent — but your audience is exactly the primed-toward-IRS, non-technical one.)
- "Audit," "review," and "analysis" are **process words** — they name what *you* do. They also read as a commoditized lead-gen opener ("everyone offers a free audit"), so they signal *sales pitch*, not *gift*.
- The highest-converting offer names use **deliverable/outcome words** (plan, game plan, blueprint) that name a finished, ownable thing the owner *gets*. Renaming a plain "consultation" to a named deliverable is a repeatedly cited 10-40% conversion lever.

**Use instead — a named, deliverable-led, time-boxed offer:**

- **"Free 15-Minute Game Plan Call"** *(safest default — outcome noun + time box + free)*
- **"The Free 15-Minute [Trade] Money-Leak Check"** *(loss-aversion hook for cautious, established owners protecting what they built)*
- **"Free 15-Minute Growth Plan Session"** *(aspiration hook for hungry, growth-stage owners)*

**Match the frame to the owner:** loss-framed ("money you're already losing") for cautious/established; gain-framed ("where you could grow") for risk-tolerant. The *fit* matters more than which one.

**Name what they walk away with on the call** (this is the deliverable that replaces "audit"):
> "In 15 minutes you'll leave with a simple 1-page plan: the one thing eating your week, what it's costing you, and the first move to fix it. No pitch, no jargon, no obligation."

## 5. Top objections + plain-language reframe scripts

Never argue the owner is wrong. Isolate the real fear, answer with a number, an everyday analogy, or a tiny reversible step.

**"I'm not techy / I'm not a tech person."**
> "If you can type a sentence, you can use this. It's closer to learning a new app on your phone than learning to code — hours, not weeks. And honestly, that's my job, not yours. You stay the expert on your business; I handle the tech side."

**"That's for the big guys — not a business like mine."** *(the single biggest non-adoption reason — ~82% of under-5-employee firms cite "not applicable")*
> "Fair — most of this stuff IS pitched to big companies. So let's not talk about AI. Tell me the one task you do every week that you hate. For a [their trade], it's usually [name it — answering after-hours calls / chasing invoices / posting on social]. That's the only thing we'd fix first." *(Lead with their use case, not a feature list.)*

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Too expensive compared to what? Most of this costs less than your monthly phone bill. The bigger number is what the problem costs you right now — let's figure that out on the call. If it's not worth it, I'll tell you."

**"I don't have time for this."**
> "No time is exactly the reason. The admin is what's eating your time. We start with the one thing stealing your evenings — and you spend 15 minutes with me once, not an hour on it every week."

**"Will it replace my people?"**
> "No — it takes the busywork off their plate so they can do the part only a human can do: build trust with your customers. You're automating your own inbox and scheduling, not firing anyone."

**"I tried ChatGPT and it gave me beige content that sounded like a polite robot."**
> "That's the most common thing I hear — and it's good news, because you already tried. It's usually not the tool, it's the brief. It's like someone who hit one Excel formula error and gave up on spreadsheets. The fix is a system, and that's what I set up for you."

**"Is this just a sales pitch? / Sounds too good to be true."**
> "Honest answer: 70-85% of AI projects fail — mostly from trying to do too much at once. So we don't. We pick ONE task, set it up, and measure it in 30 days. No pressure, no big rollout. If it doesn't move the needle, we stop." *(Behave like a legit vendor — plain language, no urgency, references on request. That IS the disarmer.)*

## 6. Trust / risk-reversal angle for a no-track-record solo

With zero case studies or logos, the owner's real fear isn't "is this good?" — it's **"will I get burned, look stupid, or waste money I can't afford?"** Win on three moves.

**A. Reverse the financial risk so "yes" costs nothing.**
- The call is **free, no obligation, no strings** — say it explicitly, because their default assumption is bait-and-switch.
- For paid work, use a **conditional (action-based) guarantee**, restate-the-promise-then-reverse-risk, and **name it**:
> "You won't pay a cent until [the first thing is live and you can see it working]." 
A named, restated guarantee reads as a real promise, not boilerplate. (Conditional is the safer default for a new high-touch service — it filters out non-doers and caps your downside. It's not that conditional "converts better"; it's the right *structure* for your ticket/risk.)

**B. Replace missing client proof with proof you control.**
- **Founder story** — lead with a *shared, relatable frustration*, not a triumph. The imperfection is what makes them trust you. ("I watched [type of owner] drown in admin while the business ran them — so I built a way to take the boring work off their plate.")
- **A demo / live thing they can see** beats any testimonial you don't have. Show the missed-call-text-back working on your own number.
- **Specificity is itself a trust signal:** exact timeframes ("live in 14 days"), exact scope ("I handle all 8 steps, you approve 2"), exact personal numbers — vague sounds like everyone, specific sounds true.
- **Authority substitutes for client logos:** the tools/partners you use, certifications, a real photo of your face (real photos beat stock).

**C. Write like a human who respects their expertise.**
- **Jargon destroys trust** — ~90% assume jargon means you're hiding that you don't know what you're talking about. Plain English throughout.
- Position yourself as **"I take responsibility for the language gap"** — you explain in plain English; they stay the expert in their own business. The grievance with agencies isn't capability, it's being made to feel stupid.
- **Invite scrutiny.** Admitting limits ("here's what I can't promise") builds more trust than a bigger promise. Reputable providers welcome being checked.

**Page placement:** stack the guarantee + "no obligation, not a sales pitch" line **directly next to the CTA button** (guarantee below the button = measurable lift). Add a grayscale trust bar (tools/certs) right after the hero to answer the subconscious "is this a scam?" Then **respond within 5 minutes** of any booking — speed-to-lead is the single biggest conversion multiplier, bigger than any on-page tweak. Embed a live calendar so they book instantly instead of waiting on an email.

---

**One-line summary of the whole playbook:** Mirror their exhausted, self-deprecating words → name the pain in plain English → promise a *finished plan they walk away with* (not an "audit") → strip out every tech word → reverse the risk to zero → and behave like the calm, jargon-free human who finally gets it.

## 1. Recommended Offer Name

**Drop "audit."** For a non-technical owner, "audit" reads like the IRS — *we'll inspect you and find what you did wrong.* It raises defenses, not desire. It's also the most overused free offer in service marketing, so it signals "this is the start of a sales pitch," not "this is a gift."

**Recommended name:**

> ### The 15-Minute "Where's My Time Going?" Call

**Option C — the plain-promise hook (clearest, safest):**
> # Get a full day of your week back — without hiring anyone.
> **Subhead:** I'm a software engineer who's spent 9 years building the behind-the-scenes systems that do repetitive work for people. Give me 15 minutes and I'll find the busywork that's costing you the most — and show you how to make it run itself.

**My pick: Option A** for the headline, **Option C's credibility line** folded into the subhead. It hits the emotion first, then earns trust with the "9 years building systems" outcome framing.

## 7. What to NOT Include

**Hard exclusions:**
- ❌ **No live demo.** Don't show the tech working. The call *is* the offer.
- ❌ **No pricing.** No prices, packages, tiers, or "starting at." Not even a range.
- ❌ **No second offer.** No newsletter signup, no lead magnet, no "or book a paid audit." One CTA only.
- ❌ **No "extensive audit" section.** Don't describe a big deep-dive process. The call is light and finite by design.
- ❌ **No nav bar / no outbound links.** Nothing that lets them leave the page without booking.

**Banned words (every one of these kills trust with a non-technical owner):**
- ❌ automation, workflow, integration, webhook, pipeline, stack, deploy, API, agent, LLM, model, GenAI, onboarding, implementation, "AI audit," "optimize your processes," "leverage," "synergy," "solutions"

**Plain-English swaps to use instead:**
- "automation" → "the boring, repeating work, done for you"
- "workflow / pipeline" → "the steps a job takes from start to finish"
- "integration" → "I connect the tools you already use so they talk to each other"
- "AI agent" → "a tireless assistant that handles a task start to finish"
- "implementation" → "I get it running for you"

**Tone rules:**
- Never make the owner feel behind, dumb, or technically illiterate.
- Lead with the *result* (time back, less stress, nothing slipping), justify with the *what* second.
- Quantify in *their* terms (a day a week, your evenings, the late invoice), not in features.
- Warm, plain, confident. Short sentences. Their words, not mine.
