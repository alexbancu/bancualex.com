# Scott Adams Writing Style

Business writing is about clarity and persuasion. The main technique: keep things simple.

Simple writing is persuasive. A good argument in five sentences sways more people than a brilliant argument in a hundred. Don't fight it.

## Core Rules

### 1. Cut Extra Words

Simple means getting rid of extra words.

Don't write "He was very happy" when you can write "He was happy." You think the word "very" adds something. It doesn't. Prune your sentences ruthlessly.

**Kill these words:**
- very, really, actually, basically, literally
- in order to → to
- due to the fact that → because
- at this point in time → now
- in the event that → if

### 2. Write Short Sentences

One thought per sentence. Readers aren't as smart as you think. Don't make them work.

Avoid putting multiple thoughts in one sentence. Complexity kills comprehension.

### 3. Grab Attention First

Your first sentence needs to hook the reader. Rewrite it until it sparks curiosity.

Adams rewrote his opening sentence a dozen times. The key is creating curiosity—making readers wonder what comes next.

### 4. Subject Before Action

Learn how brains organize ideas.

Readers comprehend "the boy hit the ball" faster than "the ball was hit by the boy." Both mean the same thing. But it's easier to imagine the object (the boy) before the action (the hitting).

All brains work this way. Use it.

**Active voice examples:**
- ❌ "The report was written by Sarah"
- ✅ "Sarah wrote the report"
- ❌ "Mistakes were made"
- ✅ "We made mistakes"

### 5. Choose Words for Effect

Humor writing is a lot like business writing. It needs to be simple. The main difference is in word choice.

For humor, don't say "drink" when you can say "swill."

Pick unexpected words that paint pictures. Vivid beats vague.

**Word upgrades:**
| Generic | Vivid |
|---------|-------|
| walk | stroll, march, trudge |
| said | admitted, snapped, muttered |
| good | sharp, solid, clean |
| bad | brutal, sloppy, weak |

## Advanced Techniques

### Write to Someone

Use the "invisible friend technique." Write to one specific person. Your writing becomes more natural and direct.

### Pace and Lead

Be like your reader in important ways. Speak like they do. Build trust first. Then lead them toward your point.

### Write to Evoke Feeling

Facts inform. Feelings persuade. Every piece of writing should make readers feel something—curiosity, urgency, relief, excitement.

### Rewrite Ruthlessly

Write it 50 times wrong to get it right once. You're not a writer if you're not rewriting.

## Checklist

Before finishing any piece:

1. Can any words be cut without losing meaning?
2. Are sentences under 15 words each?
3. Does the first sentence create curiosity?
4. Is the subject before the verb in most sentences?
5. Are there any "very," "really," or filler words?
6. Could you say this aloud without sounding awkward?
7. Would a smart 12-year-old understand it?

## The Bottom Line

That's it. You just learned 80% of the rules of good writing.

Simple. Clear. Direct. Interesting word choices.

Everything else is refinement.
